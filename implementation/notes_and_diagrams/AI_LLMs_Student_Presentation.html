LLMs Come in 3 Flavors

Think of these as three different versions of the same core technology, each optimized for different jobs:

🧩 Base Model

Raw model trained on massive text data. Can predict next words but needs fine-tuning for specific tasks.

Best for: Learning a new skill through fine-tuning

💬 Chat/Instruct

Fine-tuned to follow instructions and have conversations. Ready to use right away, faster and cheaper.

Best for: Interactive use and creative work

🧠 Reasoning/Thinking

Equipped with extended thinking. "Thinks through" complex problems step-by-step for better solutions.

Best for: Hard problem-solving

💡 Why Three Types?

Different tasks have different needs. You don't use a truck for precision work, and you don't use a sports car to move houses. Same with LLMs—choose the right tool for the job.

Base Models: The Raw Foundation

A Base Model is an LLM trained purely on predicting the next word in a sequence. It's the raw engine.

🔍 How It Works

Trained on billions of words from books, articles, code, etc. The model learns: "When I see X, Y usually follows." It's like an advanced autocomplete—type a few words, it guesses the next ones.

✅ Strengths

• Can be fine-tuned for ANY specific task
• Most flexible foundation
• Good for learning domain-specific language
• Lower inference cost baseline

❌ Limitations

• Can't follow instructions well out-of-the-box
• Might generate random or weird text
• Requires fine-tuning to be useful
• Not ready for production use

💼 Real Example

If you're building a medical AI assistant, you'd start with a Base Model, then fine-tune it on medical literature so it learns medical terminology and context.

Slide 4 of 12

Chat & Instruct Models: Ready to Talk

A Chat Model is a Base Model that's been fine-tuned to understand instructions and have conversations. It's "trained to be helpful."

🤖 How It's Different

After training on raw text, engineers give the model feedback on which responses are good and which are bad. Through this process (called RLHF - Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback), it learns to follow instructions and chat naturally.

✅ Why We Use Them

• Works immediately out-of-the-box
• Faster and cheaper than reasoning models
• Perfect for chatbots and content generation
• Safe—already aligned to be helpful

⚠️ Trade-offs

• Less flexible than Base Models
• Might hallucinate on complex topics
• Not optimized for deep reasoning
• Fixed personality from training

💼 Real Examples

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini are all Chat Models. When you ask them to "write a poem" or "explain quantum physics," that's chat model territory. Fast, practical, creative.

Reasoning Models: The Deep Thinkers

A Reasoning Model is designed to "think through" hard problems step-by-step, spending more compute upfront to get better answers.

🧠 "Budget Forcing" Explained

The model is trained with "budget forcing"—by artificially adding thinking time, it performs better at complex tasks. Like giving a student extra time on a hard exam, they can think deeper and get better answers.

✅ What You Get

• Solves hard math/logic problems
• Better coding for complex tasks
• Can reason through multi-step problems
• Higher accuracy on tough questions

⚠️ The Cost

• Slower (needs time to think)
• More expensive (more compute)
• Overkill for simple tasks
• Visible thinking (longer responses)

💼 Real Examples

When debugging complex code, solving a challenging algorithm problem, or working through a math proof—you'd want a Reasoning Model. It's worth the extra time and cost for hard problems.

Limitations: What They CAN'T Do

Frontier models are powerful, but they have real constraints:

📖 Specialized Domains

Not PhD-level in niche fields. Limited knowledge in highly specialized domains they weren't trained on.

📰 Recent Events

Knowledge cuts off at training date. Can't access current news or real-time information without external tools.

😅 Mistakes & Hallucinations

Can confidently give wrong answers. May make blind jumps to conclusions instead of reasoning carefully.

⚠️ Real Limitation

Hallucinations are tricky: When a model doesn't know something, it might make up an answer that sounds plausible. This is why you should always verify important information.

💡 Key Lesson

These models are tools, not oracles. They're best used as assistants to boost your productivity, not as the final source of truth. Always verify. Always think critically.

Choosing the Right Model for Your Task

Here's a practical decision framework:

❓ Ask Yourself These Questions

1. How complex is the task? (Simple = Chat, Complex = Reasoning)
2. What's your budget? (Reasoning is more expensive)
3. How much time do I have? (Reasoning is slower)
4. Do I need latest info? (Might need external data integration)
5. What accuracy level do I need? (Higher stakes = need better verification)

✅ Choose Chat Model When:

• Brainstorming ideas
• Writing content
• Explaining concepts
• Quick coding help
• Time is limited

✅ Choose Reasoning Model When:

• Solving complex algorithms
• Deep debugging needed
• Multi-step problem solving
• High accuracy required
• Time/cost allows

Advanced Features: Deep Research & Agents

Some models like Claude have powerful advanced capabilities:

🔬 Deep Research Mode

Claude can plan a research task, execute multiple parallel searches, fetch and analyze sources, cross-reference information, and provide a synthesized, cited report. It's like having a research assistant.

🤖 Agent Mode

Claude can take complex tasks (like "book a restaurant for 4 at 9PM in Bangalore with masala dose menu"), break them down, use tools, and execute multi-step workflows to get things done.

💼 For Your RAG Project

Deep Research could gather and synthesize information for your project automatically. Agents could handle complex multi-step workflows. These are the "next level" of LLM capabilities.

🎯 The Future

As models get better, they'll do more than chat. They'll research, plan, execute, and build. The students who understand these tools will have a significant advantage in any field.
